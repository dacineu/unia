# Trademarks and naming

## The licence does not cover the name

unia is MIT licensed. The MIT licence grants rights to **the code**. It grants
nothing to the **names and marks**: `unia`, `unia-OS`, `UPA`, `DU-UUID`,
`Mattern`, and `.ure` as a format identifier.

This matters because the licence is the weakest possible protection for a brand.
Anyone may fork, modify, and commercially redistribute the code with a single
line of attribution. Nobody may ship a competing product *called* unia. Those
are different rights, governed by trademark law, which is why they are tracked
separately here.

If your interest is building on unia, the name is not an obstacle: you can use
it in your integration documentation, you can say your product works with unia,
and you can discuss a formal trademark licence or coexistence arrangement by
emailing **<dacineu@proton.me>**.

## Permitted without permission

- Referring to the project, the software, or the `.ure` format in articles,
  talks, documentation, and tutorials
- Stating truthfully that your product integrates with, builds on, or is
  compatible with unia
- Naming a fork, such as naming your repository `unia-foo`, provided it does not
  imply official endorsement
- Using the marks in the nominative sense, meaning naming the thing you actually
  use, for example `a .ure file`

## Requires written permission

- Using `unia` or the other marks in your own product or company name
- Naming a fork or derivative in a way that implies it is the official version
- Distributing a modified build under the unia name
- Using the marks in your own marketing in a way that implies a partnership

## The symbol used in the project, and what it commits to

`(R)` appears throughout the project — in the source, the client, the
documentation, and the table above. That is a deliberate choice to mark a mark
the author intends to register, and this file is where the intent and the status
are held separately so they cannot quietly diverge.

The distinction being tracked:

- **The symbol is a statement of intent.** It says a mark is claimed for this
  name and is expected to be registered.
- **This table is the statement of fact.** `ca(R)maduci` is not cleared and not
  filed. Anyone relying on the mark commercially should read the row, not the
  symbol.

They are not the same claim, and the gap between them is the outstanding work
rather than an inconsistency. 15 U.S.C. §1125 makes false designation of goods as
registered a civil cause of action in the US, with comparable EU and UK
provisions, so the filing is what closes the gap — and it is worth completing
before the name is used commercially rather than after.

The project already leans on this file: MIT grants no protection to names, so a
mark is worth exactly nothing until it is registered. Whatever the symbol says,
the filing is what confers the right.

`ca(R)maduci` additionally has not been clearance-checked at all, which is a
separate and earlier step than filing. Search the name before printing it
anywhere public.


## Why this is filed at all

The project's value is intended to come from the specification becoming a
standard that independent implementations interoperate with, not from the
reference implementation being the only one. A trademark is not a claim over
the concept. It is a claim over the name, so that the name stays attached to
the project that earned it.

If a trademark filing would create a barrier for someone interoperating cleanly
with `.ure`, that is a signal the trademark is being applied too broadly, and it
should be narrowed.
